# Report Format

Every research deliverable follows this shape. The reader decides how deep to go; the report does
not decide for them.

## Structure

1. **TL;DR** — 3–5 lines. The answer, not a description of the work. Bold the single key term.
2. **Key findings** — one line each, with the source tier in brackets. Ordered by decision relevance,
   not by the order you found them.
3. **Detail** — one section per finding. This is where evidence, numbers and quotes live.
4. **Confidence and gaps** — what would change the conclusion, and what you could not verify.
5. **Sources** — full list with URL, publisher, date and tier.

## Rules

- **Answer first.** If the reader stops after the TL;DR, they should still have the answer.
- **Every claim carries its source** at the point of the claim, not only in the list at the end.
- **Numbers keep their units, dates and scope.** "Up 40%" is not a finding; "up 40% year over year
  in the EU market, Q1 2026, per <source>" is.
- **Separate what you found from what you concluded.** Findings are sourced. Conclusions are yours,
  and are labelled as yours.
- **Say what you did not find.** An absent answer stated plainly is a result. An absent answer
  glossed over is a defect.
- **No filler sections.** If there is no meaningful gap, write "no material gaps" and move on.

## Confidence

State one level and the reason.

| Level | Means |
|---|---|
| High | Multiple tier A/B sources agree; nothing material unverified. |
| Medium | Sourced, but thin coverage, single source on a key point, or sources partly dated. |
| Low | Mostly tier C, or the sources disagree, or the core question could not be answered directly. |

Low confidence is a legitimate deliverable. Confident phrasing over thin evidence is not.

## Length

The TL;DR and key findings fit on one screen. Detail can run as long as the evidence requires.
If the detail section is longer than about two screens, ship it with collapsible blocks or as a
separate appendix — a report nobody scrolls through is a report nobody read.
